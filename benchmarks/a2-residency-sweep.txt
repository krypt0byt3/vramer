================================================================
INTERPRETATION (2x NVIDIA A2, SM86, PCIe Gen4, 78.5 GiB host)
================================================================

1. LRU is PESSIMAL for sequential full-scan access.
   At any resident fraction < 100%, the 3-pass demand hit rate is 0.0%.
   A sequential scan larger than the cache evicts page 0 (now the LRU
   victim) exactly before the next pass needs it again -- the classic
   Belady/LRU sequential-scan pathology. For token-by-token model-layer
   streaming (scan layers 0..N every token) this means generic LRU yields
   ZERO reuse once the model exceeds the VRAM budget. => scan-aware /
   MRU-ish eviction is a real, high-value feature, not a nicety.

2. End-to-end throughput is flat at ~5.6-5.7 GiB/s regardless of resident
   fraction, and src-read time dominates the per-page cost. The pager is
   bottlenecked by the CPU source->pinned memcpy, which currently runs
   while holding the global cache mutex. => moving the source read outside
   the lock (so it overlaps H2D/compute) is the top throughput lever.

3. Page size: 64 MiB is the sweet spot on A2. 16 MiB loses ~10% to
   per-transfer overhead; 128/256 MiB give no further gain.

4. The prefetch worker now avoids thrashing (H2D traffic no longer doubles)
   and reaches high hit rates, but does not yet speed up single-pass
   streaming because the source read is lock-serialized (see #2).
